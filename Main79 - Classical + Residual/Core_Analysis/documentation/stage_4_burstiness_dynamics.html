1. The Two Features Added in Stage 4

1.1 Half-Document Jaccard Vocabulary Overlap (\(J_{\text{half}}\))

We divide the token sequence of document \(d = (t_1, \dots, t_n)\) into two equal halves: \(T_1 = (t_1, \dots, t_{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor})\) and \(T_2 = (t_{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor + 1}, \dots, t_n)\). Let \(V(T_1)\) and \(V(T_2)\) denote the sets of unique tokens in each half.

$$J_{\text{half}} = \frac{|V(T_1) \cap V(T_2)|}{|V(T_1) \cup V(T_2)|}$$

Linguistic Rationale:

  • LLM Generation (Prompt Stasis): Autoregressive models conditioned on fixed prompts and causal self-attention maintain high vocabulary continuity across the entire essay (\(J_{\text{half}} \approx 0.482\)).
  • Human Generation (Narrative Drift): Human writers progress sequentially through distinct conceptual stages (introduction \(\to\) evidence \(\to\) counterargument \(\to\) conclusion), causing genuine semantic drift and lower lexical overlap between halves (\(J_{\text{half}} \approx 0.441\), \(\Delta = -0.041\)).

1.2 Inter-Arrival Recurrence Interval Burstiness (\(B\))

In statistical physics and human dynamics (Goh & Barabási, Europhysics Letters, 2008), event arrival processes are characterized by the distribution of inter-event times.

For the top-3 most frequent content tokens in an essay, we record their 0-indexed positions \(p_1 < p_2 < \dots < p_k\). The inter-token arrival intervals are:

$$\tau_i = p_{i+1} - p_i \quad (i = 1, \dots, k-1)$$

Let \(\mu_\tau\) and \(\sigma_\tau\) denote the empirical mean and standard deviation of these intervals. The normalized burstiness parameter \(B\) is defined as:

$$B = \frac{\sigma_\tau - \mu_\tau}{\sigma_\tau + \mu_\tau}$$
Burstiness Value (\(B\)) Mathematical State Physical Process Linguistic Reality
\(B = -1\) \(\sigma_\tau = 0\) Deterministic Periodic Clock Perfect regular spacing (e.g., token every 10 words)
\(B = 0\) \(\sigma_\tau = \mu_\tau\) Memoryless Poisson Process Uniform exponential inter-arrival (LLM Softmax Sampling)
\(B > 0\) \(\sigma_\tau \gg \mu_\tau\) Heavy-Tailed Fat-Tailed Clustering Human Cognitive Recall (Bursts of reuse)

Why it is added: Human associative memory activates in bursts—once an author retrieves a concept or stylistic marker, they repeat it several times locally in close proximity, followed by long dormant stretches. Autoregressive decoders with stationary transition distributions generate text with lower burstiness closer to a memoryless Poisson process.
