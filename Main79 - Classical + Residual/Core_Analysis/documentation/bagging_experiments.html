Investigating Bootstrap Aggregation across \(M \in \{1, \dots, 20\}\) linear estimators, evaluating Breiman's stability condition on sparse hyperplanes, and resolving the severe 65% vs 35% class population asymmetry via Cost-Sensitive Balancing.

1. The Problem: Class Population Asymmetry in Empirical Risk Minimization

The corpus comprises 10,536 total documents:

  • Class B (Machine-generated, Majority): 6,837 documents (64.89%)
  • Class A (Human-authored, Minority): 3,699 documents (35.11%)

Under unweighted Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM), the support vector objective minimizes total misclassification hinge penalties:

$$\min_{\mathbf{w}, b} \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{w}\|^2 + C \sum_{i=1}^N \max(0, 1 - y_i (\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b))$$

Because Machine documents outnumber Human documents by almost 2 to 1, misclassifying a human essay incurs no greater penalty than misclassifying a machine essay. As a result, the optimal separating hyperplane shifts into the human cluster, creating a 4.76% class recall gap in our baseline:

  • Machine (Class B) Recall: 93.41%
  • Human (Class A) Recall: 88.65% (suppressed by majority population)

2. Why Standard Bootstrap Bagging Fails on Linear Models

To test whether Bootstrap Aggregation could resolve this, we swept standard BaggingClassifier(LinearSVC) across \(M \in \{1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, 20\}\) estimators.

Breiman's Stability Theorem (1996)
Bagging succeeds by reducing estimator variance: $$\text{Var}(\bar{f}) = \rho \sigma^2 + \frac{1 - \rho}{M} \sigma^2$$ where \(\sigma^2\) is base estimator variance and \(\rho\) is pairwise correlation. Bagging only produces substantial gains when base learners are unstable / high-variance (e.g. unpruned decision trees).

For convex, \(L_2\)-regularized LinearSVC with \(P \approx 253,796\) sparse N-grams:

  1. Linear hyperplanes are already low-variance, stable learners (\(\rho \approx 0.95\)).
  2. The 36.8% In-Bag Data Loss Penalty: Drawing samples with replacement means each bag contains only \(1 - 1/e \approx 63.2\%\) unique documents. Depriving a high-dimensional linear model of 36.8% of training vocabulary and examples increases estimation bias, causing accuracy to dip from 91.74% down to 91.13%.

4. Key Conclusions & Recommendations

  • The Solution to Population Asymmetry is Cost-Sensitive Weighting, Not Bagging: Applying class_weight='balanced' scales the hinge loss penalty by \(w_c = \frac{N}{2 N_c}\) (\(w_{\text{Human}} \approx 1.424, w_{\text{Machine}} \approx 0.770\)). This trains on 100% of the training data with zero sample loss, lifting accuracy to 91.89% and eliminating the human recall penalty.
  • For Ensemble Stability, Use Margin Diversity (Multi-\(C\)): Ensembling over diverse regularization penalties \(C \in [0.5, 1.5]\) achieves 91.87% with a tight standard deviation of \(\pm 0.38\%\).
