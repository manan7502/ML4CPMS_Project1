1. The Mathematics of Cumulative \(N\)-Gram TF-IDF

In text classification, higher-order \(N\)-grams capture multi-word idiomatic collocations and local phrase structures. For each token or \(n\)-gram \(t\) in document \(d\), the sublinear TF-IDF weight is defined as:

$$\text{tf-idf}(t, d) = \big(1 + \ln \text{tf}(t, d)\big) \cdot \left(\ln \frac{1 + |D|}{1 + \text{df}(t)} + 1\right)$$

where \(\text{tf}(t, d)\) is the frequency of \(t\) in \(d\), and \(\text{df}(t)\) is the document frequency across corpus \(D\). Each document vector is subsequently \(L_2\)-normalized:

$$\mathbf{x}_{\text{tfidf}}(d) = \frac{\mathbf{v}_d}{\|\mathbf{v}_d\|_2}$$

2. Why Document Length is a Vital Authorship Signal

A fundamental stylistic discrepancy between human authors and instruction-following generative language models lies in their length distributions. In this corpus:

Class Sample Count Mean Document Length (\(L_d\)) Std Dev Separation Ratio
Machine (Class B) 6,837 419.6 tokens ±164.2 1.78× Separation
Human (Class A) 3,699 747.8 tokens ±298.5

Human writers typically produce longer, meandering essays with extensive contextual development, whereas LLMs conditioned on concise prompts produce structured essays of moderate length.

Length Augmentation Impact
Appending scaled document length to cumulative 1-5 gram TF-IDF increased 5-fold CV accuracy from 88.66% to 89.23% (+0.57%), establishing our definitive baseline.

3. \(N\)-Gram Horizon Optimization: Why Stop at 5-Grams?

We conducted systematic experiments expanding the cumulative \(N\)-gram window up to 7-grams:

Configuration Features (\(P\)) Accuracy Without Length Accuracy With Length Observation
Pure 1-Gram 12,582 84.18% 85.02% Baseline unigrams
Cumulative 1-2 Gram 64,120 87.26% 87.94% Captures bigram transitions
Cumulative 1-3 Gram 142,890 88.19% 88.85% Captures trigram idioms
Cumulative 1-5 Gram (Baseline) 248,349 88.66% 89.23% Optimal capacity vs. sparsity balance
Cumulative 1-7 Gram 376,826 88.71% 89.26% Diminishing returns (+0.03%), 50% more features

Moving beyond 5-grams yields negligible gain (+0.03%) at the cost of severe sparsity and overfitting risk. Thus, Cumulative 1-5 Gram TF-IDF + Document Length (89.23%) serves as the strict reference baseline.

4. The Fundamental Limitation of Sparse Bag-of-Words

While achieving an impressive 89.23% accuracy, the TF-IDF representation suffers from intrinsic conceptual blind spots:

  1. Invariance to Vocabulary Richness: A text with 100 unique words repeated 10 times has identical nonzero coordinates to a text with 1,000 diverse words.
  2. Blindness to Subword Frequency Ranks: TF-IDF treats frequent function tokens and rare tail vocabulary symmetrically according to corpus inverse frequency, failing to capture autoregressive sampling truncation.
  3. Loss of Temporal Dynamics: Shuffling all sentences or tokens preserves the exact same TF-IDF vector, ignoring cognitive burstiness and narrative progression.

These structural shortcomings motivated the 4-stage dense feature augmentation program.
